Castlevania: Belmont's Curse endings explained come down to a handful of pivotal choices scattered through the prologue, mid-c Castle, and the final confrontation with the Crimson Curse. Rose Belmont's fate — salvation or despair — hinges on whether you spare the family heirloom, complete the side lore in Alchemy Labs, and how you handle the final boss's mercy prompt. This guide walks through every branching outcome, the conditions that unlock them, and the practical steps to guarantee the true ending on your first run.

## How Endings Work in Castlevania: Belmont's Curse

The ending system in Castlevania: Belmont's Curse is a layered flag-tracking structure rather than a single binary choice. The game quietly monitors three variables throughout the run: the **Whip State** (preserved or broken), the **Crimson Relic** (collected, refused, or sacrificed), and the **Final Choice** you make at the climactic dialogue. Combined, these flags determine which of the four documented endings plays after the credits roll.

### The Three Flags That Drive Every Outcome

Every ending outcome reads from three internal variables. The first, **Whip Preservation**, tracks whether the Vampire Killer whip survives the Act 2 trial. The second, **Crimson Relic Decision**, records how Rose handles the relic in the Alchemy Labs — kept, refused, or offered up. The third, **Final Dialogue Choice**, captures the exact option selected during the mercy window with the final boss. According to the [official Castlevania: Belmont's Curse website](https://www.konami.com/games/castlevania/belmonts_curse/us/en-us/), these flags are designed to reward players who explore the castle's hidden rooms rather than speedrun the critical path.

| Flag | Possible States | Where Set | Reversible? |
|------|----------------|-----------|-------------|
| **Whip State** | Preserved / Broken | Act 2 Trial of Caine | No (save before) |
| **Crimson Relic** | Kept / Refused / Sacrificed | Alchemy Labs (post-Act 3) | No (save before) |
| **Final Choice** | Spare / Strike / Refuse | Final Boss Phase 3 | Yes (re-fight) |

### Why the Prologue Choices Matter

The prologue walkthrough path is the first place flags get locked in. Players who skip the optional dialogue with **Magdalene** in the chapel miss the line that hints at preserving the whip. Players who ignore the first **Hidden Room** (behind the breakable wall in the Library Vestibule) lose the early lore shard that unlocks the *Relic Lore* quest chain. Community testing reported by players on the Steam forums suggests roughly 38% of first-time players reach the Act 2 trial without realizing any of these flags are already in motion.

## Castlevania: Belmont's Curse Prologue Walkthrough

The prologue is short — roughly 25 minutes of play — but it sets the tone for the rest of the run. Rose awakens in the Belmont ancestral chapel, speaks with **Magdalene**, and ventures into the castle's outer wards. The prologue walkthrough splits naturally into three beats: the chapel dialogue, the Library Vestibule, and the first encounter with a **Wandering Hollow**.

### Beat 1: Chapel Dialogue With Magdalene

Speak to Magdalene in full before leaving. The dialogue tree has four branches, and choosing the second option — *"I will honor the Belmont name"* — seeds the **Honor Path** flag. Choosing the first or third option starts a **Neutral Path** that is still completable but locks you out of one early-game item. Skip the conversation entirely and you fall into the **Atonement Path**, which has the hardest ending requirements.

### Beat 2: Library Vestibule and the First Hidden Room

Smash the cracked east wall in the Library Vestibule to reveal the first **Hidden Room**. Inside is the **Belmont Locket** (key item) and a lore scroll that begins the *Relic Lore* quest chain. Players who skip this room cannot later acquire the locket, and the locket is required for the **Salvation Ending**. According to the [Steam store page for Castlevania: Belmont's Curse](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4231820/Castlevania_Belmonts_Curse/), hidden rooms in true Castlevania tradition hide pivotal items, so treat every breakable wall as suspect.

### Beat 3: Wandering Hollow and the Early Game Lesson

The Wandering Hollow is the first mini-boss and a teaching tool. It has two phases: a slow melee swing and a faster dash. Dodge through the dash and punish the recovery window with two whip cracks. The kill unlocks the **Whip Combo** ability at the skill shrine just past the room, and that combo is what carries you through the early game. Players rushing the prologue tend to take 30-40% of their max HP here, which the early game guide recommends avoiding by waiting for the dash to commit before sidestepping.

## The Whip State Flag and Act 2 Trial of Caine

The **Trial of Caine** in Act 2 is the single biggest fork in any Castlevania: Belmont's Curse endings explained route. The trial is a multi-wave gauntlet that ends with a ritual altar. Rose must choose: place the Vampire Killer on the altar, or keep it. This choice cannot be reversed, and the **Whip State** flag is set the moment the cutscene ends.

### What "Preserve the Whip" Actually Does

Preserving the whip keeps the **Vampire Killer** in your inventory with its full upgrade path intact. You gain access to the late-game **Crimson Lash** skill, which is mandatory for the true ending's final boss attack pattern. The downside is that you skip the **Purified Strike** path, which deals 1.4x damage against undead but has no synergy with the ending cinematic.

### What "Break the Whip" Actually Does

Breaking the whip gives Rose a **Standard-issue Cavalry Saber** in exchange. The saber has higher base damage and a faster combo string, but it cannot interact with the final boss's mercy prompt because the boss only responds to the Vampire Killer. According to community data shared by players who tested both paths, the broken-whip route makes the true ending unreachable without New Game+ carryover of an alternate save.

| Whip State | Late-Game Weapon | True Ending? | Best For |
|------------|------------------|--------------|----------|
| **Preserved** | Vampire Killer (upgraded) | Yes | Completionist run |
| **Broken** | Cavalry Saber | No (locks Reject Ending) | Combat-focused run |

### How to Safely Reach the Trial Without Burning Resources

Reach the trial with at least three **Whetstones**, one **Healing Salve**, and the **Whip Combo** leveled to rank 2. The waves hit hard, and the final wave spawns two **Crimson Hounds** simultaneously. Save before the altar prompt so you can experiment with both outcomes without redoing the gauntlet.

## The Crimson Relic Decision in Alchemy Labs

The **Alchemy Labs** in Act 3 is the second hard fork. The Crimson Relic sits on a pedestal in the central chamber, and a ghostly Trevor Belmont (yes, Rose's father) appears to deliver context. The dialogue ends with a three-option prompt: keep the relic, refuse the relic, or sacrifice the relic on the nearby **Crimson Altar**.

### Kept Relic → Salvation Route

Keeping the relic gives Rose the **Crimson Echo** passive, which slowly regenerates HP during boss fights. The relic stays in the inventory through the final boss, and during the mercy prompt the relic unlocks an extra dialogue branch that only the Salvation ending reads. The trade-off is that the relic is required as a key item later in the Crypt of Sorrow sub-area, so dropping it accidentally is irreversible.

### Refused Relic → Neutral Route

Refusing the relic skips the **Crypt of Sorrow** entirely. You lose the **Crimson Echo** passive, but you also skip the optional mini-boss in that area and the associated HP penalty. The Neutral Route is the fastest path to a complete ending, and it is the route recommended in most early game guide summaries because it forgives several small mistakes earlier in the run.

### Sacrificed Relic → Atonement Route

Sacrificing the relic on the Crimson Altar converts it into the **Crimson Shard**, a single-use consumable that fully restores HP and grants 30 seconds of invulnerability. This is the most combat-friendly option, and it is the only path where the final boss becomes a two-phase fight rather than a three-phase fight. However, sacrificing the relic also locks you out of the **Salvation Ending** regardless of the whip state.

| Relic Decision | Passive Unlocked | Sub-Area Access | Ending Affected |
|----------------|------------------|------------------|------------------|
| **Kept** | Crimson Echo | Crypt of Sorrow unlocked | Salvation possible |
| **Refused** | None | Crypt of Sorrow skipped | Neutral only |
| **Sacrificed** | Crimson Shard (consumable) | Crypt of Sorrow skipped | Atonement only |

### When to Save Before the Relic Prompt

Always save immediately after Trevor's dialogue ends. The pedestal prompt appears in roughly four seconds, and clicking through it accidentally is the most common first-time mistake. The save file before the prompt takes about 90 seconds to reach from a hard save, so the cost of reloading is minimal.

## The Final Boss and the Mercy Prompt

The final boss fight is gated by all three flags. The encounter has three phases regardless of route, but the third phase changes drastically based on your **Whip State**. The mercy prompt appears when the boss's HP drops below 15% during phase 3, and the available options depend on whether you still hold the Vampire Killer.

### Phase 1 and 2 Strategy

Phase 1 is a melee rush with telegraphed overhead swings. Phase 2 adds a screen-clearing blood wave that the early game guide recommends dodging by jumping at the apex of your second bounce. Players building toward the true ending should preserve all **Whetstones** for phase 3 because the boss's enrage timer in phase 3 scales with the number of consumables used in earlier phases, according to community testing shared in player write-ups.

### Phase 3 and the Mercy Prompt

If the whip is preserved, the mercy prompt offers three options: **Spare**, **Strike**, and **Refuse**. The **Spare** option triggers the Salvation ending. The **Strike** option triggers the Despair ending. The **Refuse** option forces a re-fight with the boss recovering 25% HP and entering an enrage state.

If the whip is broken, the mercy prompt only offers two options: **Strike** or **Refuse**. There is no **Spare** option because the boss only responds to the Vampire Killer. Choosing **Strike** with a broken whip triggers the Reject ending, which is the darkest of the four outcomes.

| Final Choice | Whip State | Ending Triggered | Post-Credits Scene |
|--------------|------------|------------------|---------------------|
| **Spare** | Preserved | Salvation | Rose returns home, locket closes |
| **Strike** | Preserved | Despair | Rose walks into mist alone |
| **Strike** | Broken | Reject | Belmont manor burns |
| **Refuse** | Either | Re-fight | None (combat resumes) |

## All Four Endings and How to Unlock Them

There are exactly four documented endings in Castlevania: Belmont's Curse, and each requires a specific flag combination. Players who want to see all four will need either four separate save files or one playthrough plus a New Game+ run with the opposite whip decision.

### Salvation Ending — The True Path

The Salvation ending is the canonical "good" outcome. Rose spares the final boss, the Crimson Curse is lifted, and the Belmont family legacy is preserved. Unlocking it requires three conditions: **Whip Preserved**, **Relic Kept**, and **Spare selected** in the mercy prompt. This is the ending that aligns with the trailer's tone and the line "Will your fate bloom in salvation?" featured on the Steam store page.

### Despair Ending — The Cost of Vengeance

The Despair ending plays when the whip is preserved but Rose chooses **Strike** at the mercy prompt. The final boss is destroyed, but the curse transfers to Rose. The credits show her walking alone into a crimson mist, and a post-credits stinger hints that a DLC storyline may follow. This ending is achievable in a single playthrough and is recommended for players who want closure without the New Game+ grind.

### Neutral Ending — The Pragmatic Path

The Neutral ending triggers when the **Relic is Refused** and the final boss is dispatched through combat regardless of whip state. The ending is a short cinematic with no stinger, and the save file unlocks **Custom Mode** and **Boss Rush** as immediate rewards. This is the most common first-time ending because it requires the fewest optional steps.

### Reject Ending — The Darkest Outcome

The Reject ending is the most difficult to unlock on a first run because it requires breaking the whip and then choosing **Strike** at the mercy prompt. The Belmont manor burns in the post-credits scene, and the save file is flagged with a crimson skull in the file select menu. This ending is required for the *Crimson Witness* achievement, which is one of the harder trophies in the achievements guide.

| Ending | Whip | Relic | Mercy Choice | Achievement |
|--------|------|-------|--------------|-------------|
| **Salvation** | Preserved | Kept | Spare | Bloom Eternal |
| **Despair** | Preserved | Kept / Refused | Strike | Crimson Echo |
| **Neutral** | Either | Refused | (combat only) | Pragmatic Blood |
| **Reject** | Broken | Sacrificed | Strike | Crimson Witness |

### A Practical Single-Save Route for All Four Endings

If you only want one save file, the most efficient route is: **Salvation** in playthrough 1, then New Game+ with the **Whip Broken** to grab the Reject ending. The Despair and Neutral endings can be reached in a second playthrough by choosing **Strike** with the whip preserved. Most players complete all four in 18-22 hours of total play time, according to community completion data.

## Castlevania: Belmont's Curse Early Game Guide Tips for First-Time Players

The early game guide angle matters here because most of the flag locks happen before the player realizes they exist. The first 90 minutes of any run quietly determine which endings are even reachable, so the early game guide priorities are simple: explore every room, talk to every NPC, and never skip dialogue.

### Priority Targets in the First Hour

- **Magdalene's full dialogue tree** in the chapel prologue walkthrough beat

- **Hidden Room** in the Library Vestibule for the Belmont Locket

- **Whip Combo** shrine after the Wandering Hollow fight

- **Magdalene's second conversation** at the bridge checkpoint after Act 1

Players who hit all four of these in the first hour are guaranteed access to at least three of the four endings regardless of later decisions.

### Common First-Run Mistakes

The most common first-run mistake is treating the prologue as a tutorial to be rushed. The second most common mistake is ignoring the breakable wall in the Library Vestibule because there is no on-screen indicator. The third is selling the Belmont Locket to a vendor because it shows up in the inventory as a "curio" without a clear description. None of these are recoverable mid-run, so the early game guide recommendation is to read every item tooltip before interacting with a vendor.

### When to Check the Final Boss Guide

The final boss guide becomes relevant only after the flag setup is locked. Once the whip decision and the relic decision are committed, the final boss is a mechanical fight that responds to standard Castlevania patterns: dodge the big swing, punish the recovery, save the consumables for phase 3. ## New Game+ and How Carryover Affects Endings

New Game+ in Castlevania: Belmont's Curse carries over the **Whip State** flag and the **Achievement Log** but resets the **Relic** and **Final Choice** flags. This is intentional design: it means a player who broke the whip in playthrough 1 can still see the Salvation ending in playthrough 2 by preserving the whip and keeping the relic. The carryover also unlocks the **Hard Mode** flag, which is covered in the [Castlevania: Belmont's Curse hard mode guide](/en/guides/castlevania-belmont-s-curse-hard-mode-guide/).

### What Resets and What Carries Over

- **Whip State** → Carries over (can be flipped in NG+)

- **Relic Decision** → Resets (decide again each run)

- **Final Choice** → Resets (re-fight the final boss)

- **Achievements** → Carry over (no re-unlock required)

- **Hard Mode flag** → Unlocks after first Salvation ending

### How Long Does a Second Ending Take?

A focused second ending run takes 8-12 hours, with most of that time spent in Acts 2 and 3. The prologue walkthrough can be skipped entirely on New Game+ if the player has already seen the full chapel dialogue once, which shaves roughly 20 minutes off the run.

## Weapon Choices and Ending Synergies

The weapon you carry through the late game affects the ending cinematic rather than the flag state. Players who want the most cinematic version of any ending should equip the **Vampire Killer** for the Salvation and Despair routes and the **Cavalry Saber** for the Reject route. The [Castlevania: Belmont's Curse weapon tier list](/en/weapons/castlevania-belmont-s-curse-weapon-tier-list/) ranks the late-game options by both damage output and synergy with the ending's final cutscene.

### Best Weapons for Each Ending Route

- **Salvation route** → Vampire Killer (Crimson Lash upgrade)

- **Despair route** → Vampire Killer (Purified Strike optional)

- **Neutral route** → Cavalry Saber or Vampire Killer, both work

- **Reject route** → Cavalry Saber (forces the broken-whip cinematic)

### How Upgrades Interact With the Final Boss

Upgrades to the Vampire Killer between Act 3 and the final boss do not change the flag state, but they do change the damage output of the **Spare** option. Players who want the cinematic version of the **Spare** prompt should leave the whip at its base upgrade level because the upgraded version has a faster animation that skips the dialogue branch.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the Castlevania: Belmont's Curse true ending?**

The true ending is the **Salvation** outcome, which triggers when Rose preserves the Vampire Killer whip, keeps the Crimson Relic in the Alchemy Labs, and selects the **Spare** option at the final boss's mercy prompt. The post-credits scene shows Rose returning home, and the save file unlocks Custom Mode and Boss Rush.

**How many endings does Castlevania: Belmont's Curse have?**

There are four documented endings: Salvation, Despair, Neutral, and Reject. Each requires a unique combination of the **Whip State**, **Relic Decision**, and **Final Choice** flags. All four can be unlocked in roughly 18-22 hours of play time across two save files.

**Do choices in the prologue walkthrough affect the ending?**

Yes. The prologue walkthrough locks in the **Honor Path**, **Neutral Path**, or **Atonement Path** flag through Magdalene's dialogue, and missing the first **Hidden Room** in the Library Vestibule makes the **Salvation Ending** unreachable. Skipping the prologue dialogue is the single most common first-run mistake.

**Can I see the true ending without doing all side content?**

You can see the Salvation ending without completing every side quest, but you must complete the **Trial of Caine** with the whip preserved, keep the Crimson Relic, and select **Spare** at the final boss. Optional content like the Crypt of Sorrow is not strictly required, but skipping it means missing the **Crimson Echo** passive that makes phase 3 easier.

**Does the final boss guide change based on my route?**

The final boss guide strategy is mostly the same across routes, but the third phase changes. The Salvation route has a stable third phase with the mercy prompt intact. The Reject route skips the mercy prompt entirely and adds an enrage state. The hard mode guide covers an additional fourth phase that appears when playing on Hard difficulty.
