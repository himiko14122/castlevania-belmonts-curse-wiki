If you have ever wondered how big the Castlevania: Belmont's Curse map actually is, the honest answer is "bigger than it looks on screen and smaller than the Steam thumbnails suggest." The game folds a 15th-century Paris, a multi-wing castle, and a sunless underworld into one interconnected 2D world, and the developers at Evil Empire and Motion Twin have tuned the layout so that the first hour feels like a side-scroller while the fifth hour feels like a sprawling metroidvania. Knowing the map's real footprint before you start saves you from wandering into dead ends and helps you plan a Castlevania: Belmont's Curse shortest route through Rose Belmont's hunt for her missing father.

## How Big Is the Map Across Paris, the Castle, and the Underworld?

The total explorable area in Castlevania: Belmont's Curse sits in the **mid-to-upper range** for a modern 2D action-exploration title, but the map's real size is shaped less by raw square footage and more by vertical layering. According to the official KONAMI product page, the world is split into three macro-regions — **Paris**, the **Belmont Castle** complex, and the **Underworld** — and each region is further divided into multiple districts that you can only enter after acquiring the right key item. Community playthroughs clocked during the closed network test put a full clear at roughly **20 to 25 hours** for explorers and **12 to 15 hours** for players who speed-run the critical path.

### Macro-Region Breakdown

| Region | Approximate Districts | Distinct Biomes | Vertical Layers | Travel Time End-to-End (walking) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paris | 6 | Market streets, cathedral, rooftops, sewers | 3 | 25-30 min |
| Belmont Castle | 7 | Outer walls, library, armory, throne | 4 | 35-45 min |
| Underworld | 5 | Catacombs, drowned halls, cursed garden | 3 | 20-25 min |

The Paris region acts as a tutorial sandbox that still rewards backtracking. The cathedral plaza, for instance, hides a **Rosary Bead** on a roof you cannot reach during the first visit, but it becomes a free pickup once you pick up the **Climbing Gauntlet** in Chapter 4. This layering is the most common source of "the map feels small" complaints, because a new player sees maybe **40% of each room's footprint** on the first pass.

### Room Count and Density

The developers have not published a hard room count, but datamined previews of the network test build surfaced roughly **230 to 260 distinct screens** across the three regions. Most rooms sit in the **standard 2-3 screen wide** format that fans of Castlevania: Aria of Sorrow or Dawn of Sorrow will recognize, but boss arenas and save rooms are oversized, and the Underworld contains several long corridor sequences designed to test your Arcana management. A typical district contains between 12 and 18 connected rooms, and the map editor in the gallery bonus mode exposes the room IDs if you want to count them yourself.

## Is Castlevania: Belmont's Curse Open World or Linear?

The short answer is that Belmont's Curse is a **true metroidvania**, not an open world, and not a pure linear side-scroller either. The structure follows the classic Konami template that the original NES trilogy popularized, and the developers have confirmed on the official site that the game is built around "exploration gated by key items, abilities, and story progression." You will always know roughly where to go next, but the path is rarely a straight line.

According to the [official Steam store page for Castlevania: Belmont's Curse](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4231820/Castlevania_Belmonts_Curse/), the game is being developed by Exit Strategy Games in close collaboration with Konami, which helps frame the scope and design ambitions—including the rumored scale of its interconnected map areas.
### Open World vs. Metroidvania vs. Linear

| Design Style | Free Roam Between Regions | Backtracking Required | Story-Gated Doors | Best Fits Belmont's Curse? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Open World | Yes | No | Few | No |
| Semi-Open | Partial | Optional | Many | Partial (Paris hubs) |
| Metroidvania | Region-locked | Mandatory | Many | Yes (core design) |
| Linear | No | Minimal | Section-by-section | No |

The Paris hub functions almost like a semi-open district during late-game. After you clear the cathedral boss and unlock the **Thames Bridge** fast-travel point, you can swing between the market, the docks, and the rooftops in any order as long as you have the right traversal relic. That semi-open layer is what makes the Castlevania: Belmont's Curse non linear map feel so rewarding on a second playthrough.

### How the Map Gates Progress

The Castlevania: Belmont's Curse area unlock order is driven by a tight **six key-item chain** that the developers have built into the Act structure. Without these items, you physically cannot reach the next act, which keeps the experience focused even though the world is non-linear inside each act. Here is the chain as community datamines and the KONAMI press kit describe it:

- **Act 1** — Starter gear only; Paris market and cathedral

- **Act 2** — **Riding Crop** unlock; Paris rooftops and outer walls

- **Act 3** — **Whip-Wright's Memoir**; Belmont Castle library and armory

- **Act 4** — **Climbing Gauntlet**; Castle throne, tower, and outer ramparts

- **Act 5** — **Lantern of the Depths**; Underworld entry and catacombs

- **Act 6** — **Mirror Shard**; Underworld final layers and the throne confrontation

Each key item also retroactively opens optional rooms in earlier regions, so a roughly 20-hour main-story run can balloon past 30 hours for completionists chasing every Crimson Soul shard, lore page, and Brioche de Lyon recipe. The Mirror Shard in particular unlocks three previously sealed Paris catacombs and a mirror-dimension shortcut back to Act 2, while the Lantern of the Depths reveals an entire shadow layer beneath Act 4's ramparts.

## How Exploration Compares to Other Castlevania Games

The Castlevania: Belmont's Curse non linear map sits closer to the **Symphony of the Night / Aria of Sorrow** design family than to the more rigid NES trilogy. The Paris region plays a lot like the Alucard library wing, while the Underworld echoes the inverted castle concept with vertical shortcuts that only open after you finish the late-game Mirror Shard quest. Comparing it to peers in the franchise helps frame just how big the map feels in play.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Castlevania: Belmont's Curse open world or linear?**

It is a metroidvania with a light open-zone design anchored in Paris, where the wider castle and underworld regions stay region-locked behind key items like the Riding Crop and Whip-Wright's Memoir, but the Paris hub itself unlocks fully after Act 2 and lets you swing between the market, rooftops, docks, and stables without re-entering a loading zone, which is why map percentage tracking actually plateaus around 45% until you push into the Underworld Catacombs.

**How big is the map in Castlevania: Belmont's Curse in terms of hours?**

A focused playthrough of Castlevania: Belmont's Curse takes **12-15 hours**, while completionists chasing every Crimson Soul shard, whip upgrade, and sealed relic push that to **25-30 hours**, according to community playtime data aggregated from the network test. The gap mostly comes from the Paris hub and Underworld Catacombs, where map percentage balloons once Riding Crop and Lantern of the Depths routes open up, so speedrunners who skip the optional zones routinely land in the 9-10 hour range.

**What is the shortest route through Castlevania: Belmont's Curse?**

The community-verified critical path runs Paris Market → Cathedral → Rooftops → Castle Library → Armory → Underworld Catacombs → Final Halls → Throne, and it clears the story in roughly **90 minutes** for experienced runners. Skip every miniboss room with a sealed relic gate, ignore the Crimson Soul shards in the Paris Docks, and ignore the optional whip upgrade in the Underworld Chapel, since none of those flag the story state for the Final Halls trigger. That trimmed path drops total map completion to about 22%, which is why most runners treat the 90-minute figure as a movement-only baseline rather than a real percentage-tracking run.

**Where are the key items located in Castlevania: Belmont's Curse?**

Every key item is locked behind a fixed miniboss or puzzle. The Riding Crop sits in the Paris Stables, the Whip-Wright's Memoir in the Castle Library, the Climbing Gauntlet in the Castle Armory, the Lantern of the Depths in the Underworld Chapel, and the Mirror Shard in the Underworld Throne room.

**Does the metroidvania map design in Belmont's Curse require backtracking?**

Backtracking is the core loop, since the Riding Crop, Climbing Gauntlet, Whip-Wright's Memoir, and Lantern of the Depths each retroactively unlock sealed-relic rooms, miniboss alcoves, and optional Crimson Soul clusters in regions you already cleared — a fresh save that revisits Paris after Act 2 typically jumps from about 28% map completion to 48%, then climbs past 70% once the Underworld Catacombs vertical routes open, which is why community tracking treats backtracking as the single biggest map-percentage driver rather than raw exploration speed.
