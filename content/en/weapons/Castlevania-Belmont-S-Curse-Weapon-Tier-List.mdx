## S-Tier: Whip and Sword Families

The two families that anchor every successful Belmont's Curse build are the **Vampire Killer whip line** and the standard **Sword** line, and the gap between them is narrower than tier-list infographics suggest once you factor in whip reach against Sword swing arcs. Pick the whip if you're stacking Holy Water sub-weapons and need the 1.2× reach multiplier to hit around arena pillars in the Clock Tower and Colosseum sub-rooms; pick the Sword if you prefer tighter hitboxes for stun-locking bosses like the Death sequence, since the +8% critical rate on fully upgraded Blade sells more raw damage per second than the whip's Holy Water synergy. Both answer different questions about your playstyle, and both unlock on the same vertical progression wall, so the choice usually comes down to sub-weapon route rather than weapon power.

### Vampire Killer and the Whip Family

The Whip is the single most important weapon family in the game, and the Castlevania: Belmont's Curse Vampire Killer behaves very differently from the standard whips. According to the official description, the whip's role is dual-purpose: it functions as a primary attack and as a swing-and-reposition tool that lets Rose Belmont glide between platforms, which means Castlevania: Belmont's Curse whip guide content always emphasizes that you are not just holding a melee weapon, you are holding a movement tool. The Castlevania: Belmont's Curse best early weapon for most players is the **Leather Whip** picked up in the prologue, and it remains relevant deep into Act 2 because the aerial swing mechanic lets you skip entire enemy formations on the way to the next lantern.

The Vampire Killer line scales the hardest of any weapon category in the late game. The Castlevania: Belmont's Curse best late game weapon slot is almost always held by the **Awakened Vampire Killer**, which unlocks after the midpoint boss chain. Its late-game form upgrades both the swing speed and the swing-arc, and it carries the highest physical DPS in the game once fully upgraded. If you have to pick one weapon to invest Castlevania: Belmont's Curse weapon upgrades in, it is this one.

### Sword Family

Swords sit in S-tier because they offer the cleanest balance of damage, combo speed, and Castlevania: Belmont's Curse weapon upgrades that scale well into the late game. While swords do not give you the aerial swing of the whip, they make up for it with a faster combo string and a wider swing arc on the heavy attack, which is the most reliable answer to the Paris street fodder encountered in the early game.

The **Rapier** sub-line is the Castlevania: Belmont's Curse best early weapon for players who prefer a sword playstyle, and it stays relevant through most of Act 2. For the Castlevania: Belmont's Curse best late game weapon slot in the sword family, the **Flame Brand** unlocked from the late-game cathedral chest is the strongest option, with built-in fire Arcana synergy that dovetails with the [best Arcana combo loadouts](/en/arcana/castlevania-belmont-s-curse-best-arcana-combo/) documented elsewhere on the site.

## A-Tier: Spear and Knuckle Families

The A-tier represents weapons that are not always the top pick but are strong enough to main for an entire act, especially when their kit matches a specific boss or zone. In the Spear family, the Partisan in particular shines against aerial and large-body bosses like Shaft and Dracula's first form, where its extended reach and multi-hit thrust windows let you build aura faster than most Knuckle weapons. The Knuckle family trades that range for stagger potential, with the Raging Fists and Iron Fist variants delivering frame-tight combos that melt the Axe Knight line and Carmilla in the shorter corridors of the inverted castle. Because both families unlock through relatively cheap upgrade chains compared to S-tier whips like the Vampire Killer, they are a practical main for a first Blind run, and they double as solid backups when you swap back to your whip for a phase-2 finisher.

### Spear Family

Spears have the longest reach in the game, which is a huge advantage in the cramped corridors of the Paris streets. The Castlevania: Belmont's Curse weapon unlocks inside the spear family include the **Iron Spear** in Act 1 and the **Partisan** in Act 2, and both are excellent answers to enemies that hang back at the edge of a room. The Castlevania: Belmont's Curse weapon upgrades for spears tend to favor the **thrusting-heavy** playstyle, so if you prefer poking rather than swinging, spears are a legitimate main weapon for the first half of the run.

The main trade-off is that spears have the slowest swing recovery in the game, so getting hit during the recovery frames is a real risk in late-game boss fights. By Act 3, most players will have switched to the **Awakened Vampire Killer** or the **Flame Brand**, but the spear remains the best answer to certain mini-bosses that demand reach.

### Knuckle Family

Knuckles are the fastest weapon in the game, with the highest attacks-per-second and the lowest stamina cost per swing. They are an excellent pairing with Arcana-heavy builds, because the rapid swing rate triggers Arcana effects more often than slower weapons. The Castlevania: Belmont's Curse best early weapon in the knuckle family is the **Iron Fist**, which you can pick up almost immediately after the first save point, and it remains a strong Act 1 main weapon.

Knuckles fall to A-tier rather than S-tier because their short reach makes them awkward in late-game boss fights, where the answer is usually to hit-and-run rather than mash. Pairing knuckles with relics that extend combo reach can push them toward S-tier, which is why the [relic upgrades guide](/en/relics/castlevania-belmont-s-curse-relic-upgrades/) is worth reading once you have decided on a knuckle main.

| Partisan | Acts 2-3 | Thrust DPS | Limited arc |

## B-Tier: Axe, Throwing, and Polearm Families

B-tier weapons are situationally powerful but rarely worth maining for an entire act. They tend to shine during one specific window of the run or against one specific enemy type, then fall off hard once better options are unlocked.

### Axe Family

Axes hit the hardest per swing of any weapon in the game, but they are slow and stamina-hungry. The **Battle Axe** is the most useful axe in the early game, and it is the Castlevania: Belmont's Curse best early weapon for players who want a heavy-swing playstyle. Once you have access to the **Great Axe** in Act 2, it becomes a top-tier option for armored enemies, which are otherwise very tedious to fight with a sword or whip.

The reason axes drop to B-tier rather than climbing to A-tier is that the **Awakened Vampire Killer** and **Flame Brand** out-DPS them in nearly every late-game scenario, and the slow swing speed makes axes a liability in fast boss fights. Keep an axe in your inventory for armored mini-bosses, but do not invest Castlevania: Belmont's Curse weapon upgrades into axes past Act 2.

### Throwing Family

Throwing weapons are the only ranged physical option in the game, and they are the answer to a specific problem: the prologue section where you cannot yet use the whip to clear ledges or pick off snipers. The Castlevania: Belmont's Curse weapon unlocks in the throwing family are cheap and abundant, which is why the [weapons overview](/en/weapons/castlevania-belmont-s-curse-weapons/) treats them as utility items rather than primary weapons.

By the time you reach Act 2, throwing weapons are outclassed by every other category, and they become an emergency panic-button tool rather than a main weapon. Do not invest Castlevania: Belmont's Curse weapon upgrades into throwing weapons past the first hour.

### Polearm (Non-Spear) Family

The remaining polearm-type weapons are a small category of slow, long-reach options that occupy a weird design space between spears and axes. They have a niche use in the late-game crowd-control sections, where their wide swing arc can clear a wave of weaker enemies in one motion, but they are not strong enough to recommend as a main weapon. The Castlevania: Belmont's Curse best late game weapon slot is never a polearm, but it is worth knowing the category exists if you enjoy an unconventional playstyle.

## Strategy: Choosing the Right Weapon for Each Stage

The Castlevania: Belmont's Curse weapon tier list is most useful when paired with a clear plan for which weapon to main at each stage of the run. The following walkthrough assumes a standard first playthrough, with reference points to the [mid-game guide](/en/guides/castlevania-belmont-s-curse-mid-game-guide/) for boss-specific tips.

### Prologue and Act 1

The first hour of the game is designed to teach you the whip's dual role as both a weapon and a movement tool. The Castlevania: Belmont's Curse best early weapon during this window is whatever weapon is given to you by the game's tutorial, which is the **Leather Whip**. Do not switch off it until you have mastered the aerial swing, because the Castlevania: Belmont's Curse whip guide mechanics that you learn here will define your entire mid-game combat flow.

Once you reach the first save point, the recommended main is the **Rapier** for sword players or the **Iron Fist** for knuckle players, paired with the **Thorn Whip** as a movement tool. Save the **Iron Spear** for ranged anti-air situations in the Paris streets.

### Act 2 and Mid-Game

By Act 2, you should have access to the **Vampire Killer** itself, which is the first real Castlevania: Belmont's Curse weapon upgrade that gives you a major power spike. The Castlevania: Belmont's Curse best mid-game weapon loadout is the **Vampire Killer** as your main and the **Partisan** as your ranged poke for boss adds. This is also the point where the Castlevania: Belmont's Curse weapon upgrades investment should start to ramp up, because the late-game whip forms require upgrade materials that only drop from Act 2 and Act 3 enemies.

### Act 3 and Late Game

The Castlevania: Belmont's Curse best late game weapon is almost always the **Awakened Vampire Killer** for physical builds or the **Flame Brand** for Arcana-heavy builds. Both are unlocked through the late-game cathedral chain, and both are worth saving upgrade materials for during the entire mid-game.

A common mistake is to spread Castlevania: Belmont's Curse weapon upgrades across multiple weapons rather than focusing on one. Because the late-game weapons have such a high upgrade ceiling, the optimal strategy is to main one weapon from Act 2 onward and dump all upgrade materials into it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the best weapon in Castlevania: Belmont's Curse?**

The **Awakened Vampire Killer** is the best weapon in Castlevania: Belmont's Curse once fully upgraded, because it has the highest physical DPS ceiling, retains the aerial swing utility, and is the most accessible late-game unlock. For players running an Arcana-heavy build, the **Flame Brand** sword is a strong alternative that trades some raw DPS for fire proc synergy.

**What is the best early weapon in Castlevania: Belmont's Curse?**

The **Leather Whip** is the best early weapon because the game gives it to you in the prologue, and its aerial swing mechanic is essential for navigating the Paris streets until you reach the first save point. The **Rapier** is the best early-game main for sword players who prefer faster combos, and the **Iron Fist** is the best early-game main for Arcana-heavy builds that benefit from high swing speed.

**How do I unlock the Vampire Killer in Belmont's Curse?**

The **Vampire Killer** is unlocked through a story-locked drop from the Act 2 boss chain, which is the same path that leads into the cathedral. Once unlocked, it can be upgraded through the normal weapon upgrade system, and the **Awakened Vampire Killer** late-game form requires materials dropped from Act 3 enemies.

**Are there any weapons worth skipping in the tier list?**

Yes, the **toothpick and curio** weapon category is essentially tutorial-only filler that becomes irrelevant by the time you reach the first save point. The **throwing** weapon family is also a skip for most players past Act 1, because every other weapon category out-DPSes it once proper melee weapons are unlocked. Invest your Castlevania: Belmont's Curse weapon upgrades in S-tier and A-tier families rather than B-tier or below.

**Should I respec my weapon upgrades in Castlevania: Belmont's Curse?**

No, the game does not offer a free weapon respec system, so once you commit Castlevania: Belmont's Curse weapon upgrades to a specific weapon, those materials are spent. The recommended strategy is to pick a single S-tier main weapon by the end of Act 1 and commit to it for the rest of the run, which is why the [endings guide](/en/guides/castlevania-belmont-s-curse-endings-explained/) recommends maining the **Awakened Vampire Killer** for the true ending route.
