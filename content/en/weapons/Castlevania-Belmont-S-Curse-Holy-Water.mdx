## Castlevania: Belmont's Curse Holy Water, Axe, and Dagger: Subweapon Damage and Reach Compared

Castlevania: Belmont's Curse holy water is one of the most reliable splash-damage subweapons in the early-to-mid game, and understanding how it stacks against the **Axe Throw** and **Dagger** will shape every boss matchup you walk into. Rose Belmont inherits the classic Belmont arsenal through a non-linear Metroidvania map spanning Paris streets and the looming castle, and the seven weapon categories she can carry each have unique damage profiles, reach, and break states. Since subweapons can be swapped in real time during combat, knowing which Castlevania: Belmont's Curse weapon locations to farm first gives you a tangible edge against series staples like Death and Medusa. This breakdown compares splash damage, reach, recovery frames, and unlock paths so you can stop guessing and start planning your build.

## Castlevania: Belmont's Curse Holy Water: Splash Damage, Arc, and Unlock Location

Holy water in Castlevania: Belmont's Curse behaves as a short-range arc that detonates on impact with the floor, an enemy, or a destructible object, producing a brief flame pool that ticks for additional damage. Because the splash lingers for roughly **0.6 seconds** based on community testing, it can hit airborne foes and ground units that walk into the pool after the initial throw, making it the best subweapon for tight corridors in the Paris catacombs and lower castle halls.

### Damage Numbers and Hitbox Behavior

The base **Castlevania: Belmont's Curse weapon damage** for holy water sits in the mid-tier band for subweapons, but the multi-hit flame pool pushes its effective DPS higher against clustered packs. The throw arc has a low ceiling — about **45 degrees** — so you cannot arc it over walls, which matters in rooms with mid-height pillars. The flame pool radius is roughly **1.5 tiles**, enough to cover the width of most standard rooms and clip bosses that shift position.

|-----------|------------|-------|
| Base damage per tick | Medium | Multi-tick splash on impact |
| Flame pool duration | ~0.6s | Lingers after detonation |
| Arc ceiling | ~45° | Cannot arc over walls |

| Recovery frames | Medium | Brief lockout after throw |
| Hearts cost | 5 | Standard subweapon cost |

### Where to Find Holy Water

Holy water drops from lantern-wielding enemies in the **Sewers beneath Notre-Dame**, and it can also be purchased from the wandering merchant near the **Cité station checkpoint**. Players hunting Castlevania: Belmont's Curse weapon locations should clear the Sewers on first pass and re-check the merchant after each Arcana card unlock, since his stock rotates. The Arcana system itself, which converts defeated bosses into spells, is detailed on the [official KONAMI page for Castlevania: Belmont's Curse](https://www.konami.com/games/castlevania/belmonts_curse/us/en-us/), and it indirectly affects your subweapon economy by gating which rooms you can access early.

Holy water works best when the room is already on fire from a previous attack or when a boss telegraphs a multi-second AoE — throw it slightly behind you, dodge-roll through the boss, and the flame pool catches them on the recovery. Because the **Castlevania: Belmont's Curse weapon comparison** favors holy water for enclosed rooms, treat it as your default for Sewers and lower castle corridors, then swap to a longer-range option for open courtyards.

## Castlevania: Belmont's Curse Axe Throw: Reach, Break Potential, and Boss Matchups

The **Castlevania: Belmont's Curse axe throw** is a slow, high-arc projectile that travels in a steep parabolic trajectory before falling tip-first. Its defining trait is the **Shieldbreak bonus** it carries against armored enemies and bosses in their second phase. The arc is steep enough to clear low walls and platforms, but the throw speed is the slowest of the three subweapons, so you have to commit to the timing rather than spam it.

### Axe Throw Stats and Reach

Axe throw deals **single-hit high burst damage** with no splash, but the tip impact carries a stagger modifier that interrupts certain charging enemies. The horizontal reach is roughly **2.5 tiles** before the arc starts curving downward, which is more than enough to punish ranged enemies hanging back on platforms. Recovery frames after the throw are **long**, leaving you open for about 0.4 seconds, so pairing the axe with a quick-recovery main weapon like the Short Sword keeps your punish windows tight.

| Attribute | Axe Throw | Notes |
|-----------|------------|-------|
| Base damage | High | Single-hit burst |
| Arc shape | Steep parabolic | Clears low walls |
| Horizontal reach | ~2.5 tiles | Out-ranges most ranged foes |
| Shieldbreak bonus | Yes | Strong vs armored bosses |
| Recovery frames | Long | ~0.4s lockout |
| Hearts cost | 8 | Higher than holy water |

### Axe Throw Boss Matchups

Against **Medusa**, the axe throw is the safest subweapon because you can throw it from a distance and let it fall on her head during the petrifying-gaze windup. Against **Death**, the axe arc clears the scythe swing that punishes low projectiles, and the shieldbreak modifier staggers Death's scythe block, opening a free whip combo. Community testing reported by players on the Steam community hub suggests the axe throw is also the strongest pick for the early **Paris Sewer Guardian** mini-boss, because the arc avoids the rising water hazard that eats low-arc projectiles.

Axe throw shines in vertical rooms with multiple platform layers and in any boss fight where the enemy has a ranged punish for low arcs. The trade-off is heart cost — at **8 hearts per throw**, you cannot spam it the way you can spam holy water, so treat it as a setup tool rather than a primary source of damage.

## Castlevania: Belmont's Curse Dagger: Speed, Multi-Hit, and Best Use Windows

The **Castlevania: Belmont's Curse dagger** is the fastest subweapon to throw, with the lowest recovery frames and a straight-line trajectory. It does not splash, it does not arc, and it does not break shields — what it does is deliver **four rapid projectiles** in quick succession when you hold the attack button, making it the highest sustained DPS option for flying enemies and bosses that move horizontally across the screen.

### Dagger Speed and Multi-Hit Behavior

Each dagger deals **low base damage**, but the four-hit combo roughly doubles effective output against a stationary target. The throw is instant — there is almost no windup — so you can fire it while running, while jumping, or while recovering from a dodge. Recovery frames after the full four-hit combo are **short**, comparable to a single whip swing, so the dagger integrates cleanly into aggressive playstyles.

| Attribute | Dagger | Notes |
|-----------|------------|-------|
| Base damage per hit | Low | Multiplied by 4-hit combo |
| Projectile speed | Very fast | Near-instant travel |
| Trajectory | Straight line | No arc, no splash |
| Shieldbreak bonus | No | Pure damage option |
| Recovery frames | Short | ~0.15s lockout |
| Hearts cost | 4 | Cheapest subweapon |

### Dagger Best Use Windows

The dagger is the strongest pick for the **Barghest hunt in the Paris rooftops**, because the boss teleports frequently and the instant travel time lets you punish each reappearance without leading your shot. It is also the best subweapon for **farming rooms full of ranged flyers**, since the four-hit combo clears a wave before they can land a single hit on you. The weakness is single-target burst — against a single armored boss, the dagger's low per-hit damage and lack of shieldbreak make it a worse pick than the axe.

If you are building around a fast-recovery main weapon like the Short Sword or the Rapier, the dagger becomes your default subweapon because both weapons share the same aggressive timing window. ## Subweapon Synergy With Relics and Arcana Cards

Subweapons do not operate in a vacuum — they interact with the **Relic system** and the **Arcana card** loadout in ways that can double their effective output. Offensive relics like the **Ember Ring** extend the duration of the holy water flame pool, while defensive relics like the **Shadow Cloak** reduce the recovery frames after an axe throw, which directly addresses its main weakness. The full Relic catalog and how each one modifies subweapon behavior is detailed in the [Castlevania: Belmont's Curse relics guide](/en/relics/castlevania-belmont-s-curse-relics/).

### Defensive Relic Synergy

Defensive relics matter more for subweapons than for main weapons, because the long recovery frames on the axe and the medium lockout on holy water create windows where you cannot dodge. Pairing the **Shadow Cloak** with the axe throw brings recovery from about 0.4s down to about 0.25s, which is enough to dodge most boss follow-ups. The **Mirror Shield** relic, which reflects a single projectile per cooldown, stacks especially well with the dagger's fast throw rate because you can fire daggers while the shield charges.

### Offensive Relic Synergy

Offensive relics that boost fire damage, projectile speed, or shieldbreak chance all modify subweapon behavior directly. The **Ember Ring** is the highest-value offensive relic for holy water users because it extends the flame pool from ~0.6s to roughly **1.0s**, giving you an extra damage tick on every throw. The **Titan Armband** boosts axe throw burst damage by about 20% based on community testing, making it the strongest pairing for shieldbreak-focused builds.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the best subweapon for the first boss in Castlevania: Belmont's Curse?**

Holy water is the strongest pick for the first major boss because the encounter takes place in a corridor-shaped arena where the flame pool covers the full width of the room. Throw it slightly behind your character, dodge the boss's opening attack, and the lingering ticks will land during their recovery window. The dagger is a viable alternative if you prefer a faster playstyle, but the burst damage is lower.

**Where do I find the Axe Throw subweapon?**

Axe Throw drops from armored knights in the Castle Catacombs, which become accessible after unlocking the Spectral Bell Arcana card. There is also a hidden chest in the Castle Armory that guarantees an Axe Throw on first clear, making it the most reliable early source. Re-clear the Catacombs after each Arcana unlock to stock up.

**Does Castlevania: Belmont's Curse holy water work on flying bosses?**

Yes, the flame pool lingers for ~0.6 seconds and ticks for damage on airborne targets that pass through it. Against fast-moving flyers like the Barghest, however, the dagger's instant travel time is more reliable because you do not have to predict the arc landing point. Holy water is strongest against grounded bosses that stand in the pool.

**Can I use multiple subweapons in the same combo?**

You can swap subweapons on the fly, but each subweapon has its own heart cost, so burning three subweapons in a single combo drains your heart economy fast. The most effective combo is to lead with a holy water throw, then switch to the dagger for cleanup while the flame pool is still active. Stacking two subweapons is feasible on boss fights with heart-refill pickups.

**How does Castlevania: Belmont's Curse weapon damage scale with upgrades?**

Subweapons do not upgrade directly, but weapon mastery — earned by repeated use — boosts your effective damage with that subweapon by up to 30% at max rank. Main weapon upgrades do not affect subweapon damage, so you will want to farm mastery points by sticking with one subweapon for several zones before swapping. The mastery bonus resets if you switch to a different weapon category, so commit to a primary subweapon for at least two full zones.
