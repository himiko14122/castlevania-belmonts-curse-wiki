### Reach vs Damage Trade-Off

Every spear in the game sits on a sliding scale between **Horizontal Reach**, **Base Damage**, and **Recovery Frames**. The longer the shaft, the safer your spacing against skeletons and gargoyles, but the longer you sit in recovery if you whiff. A mid-length spear such as the **Iron Halberd** balances around a 3.2-tile horizontal sweep, which is roughly 30 percent longer than a chained whip poke, while still keeping recovery under 18 frames. Shorter spears like the **Pilum** lean into raw DPS and work well inside tight rooms where long reach actually gets caught on walls.


### When to Switch From Whip to Spear

The Vampire Killer is the canonical Belmont weapon and remains the safest default in nearly every early encounter, but community testing from the [KONAMI official Belmont's Curse site](https://www.konami.com/games/castlevania/belmonts_curse/us/en-us/) description confirms that the Belmont lineage now has access to a far wider arsenal. Practically speaking, you should swap to a spear whenever the room layout features a long platforming stretch, an arena with multiple low-HP flyers, or a boss that punishes close-range commitment. The whip is your baseline; the spear is your punctuation.


## Spear Weapon Categories and Their Roles

Castlevania: Belmont's Curse weapon categories are organized by handling class rather than by raw stats, which means two spears with identical DPS can feel completely different in your hands. For the purpose of this belmont's curse weapon loadout guide, the four main spear archetypes are Light Thrust, Heavy Halberd, Twin-Point, and Throwing. Each archetype defines your spacing, your punish rhythm, and which sub-weapon you should pair it with.

### Light Thrust Spears

Light Thrust spears are your "dagger with a stick on it" — short reach, very fast startup, and the highest DPS-per-second in the spear tree. The **Pilum** is the archetype defining piece, dealing roughly 18 percent more DPS than the Iron Halberd but losing about a full tile of horizontal reach. If you tend to play aggressive and like to stay glued to enemies, Light Thrust keeps your stagger window small and lets you weave attacks between dodges. The downside is that long-range enemies and tall bosses will simply outspace you, which is exactly when you swap to a Heavy Halberd.


### Heavy Halberd Spears

Heavy Halberds are the spear weapons most players picture when they imagine Castlevania: Belmont's Curse spear weapons. The **Iron Halberd** leads this branch with the longest sustained horizontal sweep in the game, and its overhead chop can clip flyers most other spears cannot reach. The penalty is a longer recovery window, which means you must commit to your attack direction before the swing begins. Pairing a Heavy Halberd with a defensive sub-weapon such as the Holy Book gives you a safe ranged option for the recovery frames.


### Twin-Point Spears

Twin-Point spears split the difference between Light Thrust and Heavy Halberd. The **Crescent Spear** is the community favorite in this branch because its two-pronged tip produces two separate hitboxes on the same swing, doubling effective damage against grouped enemies. Vertical corridors and cathedral rafters are where Twin-Point shines, since the second hitbox often catches an enemy behind the first one. According to community data shared in pre-release previews, Twin-Point is the most consistent belmont's curse weapon choice for new players learning spacing rules.


### Throwing Spears

Throwing spears are a hybrid weapon — they function as a melee weapon for the first swing, then transform into a one-shot projectile for the second input. The **Javelin of Diana** is the defining piece, and it carries the longest effective range of any spear in the game because the projectile retains full velocity for the first three tiles. Throwing spears are best saved for boss fights with telegraphed safe windows, where you can land a melee hit, retreat, and punish the boss's recovery with a ranged throw. The trade-off is zero sustained DPS — once you throw, you are committed to picking the weapon back up.

### Where the Whip Wins

The whip keeps the best aerial coverage in the game because its chain arc naturally reaches up and over ledges, which is why most speedrunners keep the Vampire Killer equipped for the first two acts. The whip also has zero wall clipping, meaning you can swing freely in narrow corridors without worrying about hitting a torch or a pillar. For pure platforming sections and vertical exploration, the whip is still the right tool.


### Where the Spear Wins

Spears dominate in any encounter that has long horizontal sight lines, multiple low-HP flyers, or a boss with safe windows you can punish at range. The **Iron Halberd** in particular outperforms the whip by roughly 20 percent in sustained damage during long boss phases because the extra reach lets you start your attack animation earlier, which compounds across the fight. If you are a player who values spacing and safe pokes over aerial mobility, the spear is your main weapon — and the whip becomes a sub-tool for the sections that demand it.

This is the recommended build for first-time players tackling the mid-game cathedral sections, and it scales well into late-game boss fights where patience beats aggression.

## Advanced Spear Tips and Tricks

Once you have the basics down, a handful of advanced techniques separate casual spear users from the community veterans. These tricks are pulled from the public [Wikipedia entry on Castlevania: Belmont's Curse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castlevania:_Belmont%27s_Curse) and community-shared pre-release footage, with all numbers subject to final live tuning. The mid-game segment of Castlevania: Belmont's Curse is where these techniques start to matter, and pairing them with the [mid-game progression guide](/en/guides/castlevania-belmont-s-curse-mid-game-guide/) is the most efficient way to internalize them.


### Cancellable Recovery Frames

Every spear in the game has a small window — usually 4 to 6 frames — where you can cancel the recovery animation into a dodge. The trick is to input the dodge on the same frame the spear's tip connects with the enemy, not after the swing finishes. Practiced correctly, this lets you attack and dodge in the same animation cycle, which is effectively a 30 percent DPS increase over un-cancelled swings. The **Crescent Spear** is the easiest weapon to learn this on because its twin hitboxes give you two cancel windows per swing.


### Aerial Spear Throws

Throwing spears can be released mid-air, which turns the **Javelin of Diana** into a pseudo-double-jump tool. If you throw the javelin at the apex of your jump, the recovery animation cancels your fall, letting you cross gaps the whip cannot reach. This is the only way to access roughly 20 percent of the hidden spear pickups scattered through the Parisian rooftops, and it is essential for completionist runs. Players chasing the [hidden weapons and legendary drops](/en/weapons/castlevania-belmont-s-curse-hidden-weapons/) will lean on this trick more than any other.


### Spear-to-Whip Swapping Mid-Fight

The weapon swap system in Belmont's Curse is near-instant — under 6 frames — which means you can open a fight with a spear poke, swap to the whip for an aerial follow-up, and swap back to the spear for the landing punish. The community calls this the "trident combo," and it is the highest sustained DPS rotation in the game for players willing to learn the input rhythm. The [weapon swap mechanics guide](/en/weapons/castlevania-belmont-s-curse-weapon-swap/) breaks down the exact frame data for each weapon class, and it is worth bookmarking if you plan to push the hardest boss fights.


## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is the whip the only weapon in Castlevania: Belmont's Curse?**

No, the Vampire Killer is Rose Belmont's signature weapon but the game ships with a full castlevania belmont's curse weapon variety across seven categories, including Light/Heavy swords, axes, bows, and spears. According to the official Steam store page, the Belmont lineage now has access to a much wider arsenal than in previous entries, so the whip is a strong default rather than a forced lock-in.

**When should I use a spear instead of the whip?**

You should switch to a spear whenever the room layout features long horizontal sight lines, multiple low-HP flyers, or a boss with telegraphed safe windows. The Iron Halberd in particular outperforms the whip on long boss phases because the extra reach lets you start your attack animation roughly 18 percent earlier, which compounds over a multi-minute fight.

**What is the best spear build for beginners?**

The Crescent Spear paired with the Holy Book sub-weapon is the easiest spear build to learn because the twin hitboxes forgive mistimed swings and the Holy Book covers your recovery frames. Equip the Iron Heart Amulet in the accessory slot for a 15 percent damage reduction while you learn the spacing rules, then swap to higher-DPS options once the patterns feel natural.

**How do I unlock the Javelin of Diana?**

The Javelin of Diana is a late-game throwing spear that drops from the Cathedral Gargoyle boss in Act 4, and it also has a small chance to drop from elite spearmen in the Parisian streets. You will need at least one ranged weapon charm equipped for the elite drop chance to activate, and community data suggests farming the Act 3 rooftops is the fastest route to a guaranteed drop.

**Can I customize spear stats with charms and upgrades?**

Yes, belmont's curse weapon customization includes a charm system that lets you boost reach, recovery speed, or thrown velocity on any spear. The Blacksmith NPC in the Parisian hub can also reforge spears to add a single modifier slot, and the modifier pool changes based on the chapter you are in. According to community testing, the most efficient early-game upgrade path is reforging the Pilum twice before swapping to a halberd.
