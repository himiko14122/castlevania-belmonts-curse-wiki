When Konami revealed a brand-new 2D Castlevania during its 40th anniversary showcase, the most surprising name on the credits sheet wasn't a veteran Japanese action studio — it was Evil Empire, the French team best known for years of Dead Cells DLC drops. Castlevania: Belmont's Curse, the franchise's 26th mainline entry, is being co-developed by Evil Empire with Motion Twin consulting, and published by Konami for an October 15, 2026 launch on PC, PS5, Xbox Series X|S, and Switch. The Evil Empire involvement matters because it telegraphs exactly how this Metroidvania is going to feel: snappy, run-tuned combat, layered progression, and a build-craft loop lifted straight out of the Dead Cells playbook.

## Why Evil Empire Is the Studio Behind Castlevania: Belmont's Curse

### From Dead Cells DLC Factory to Full Co-Developer

Evil Empire built its reputation as the long-running support studio for Dead Cells, where it shipped wave after wave of paid expansions — *Rise of the Giant*, *The Bad Seed*, *Fatal Falls*, *The Queen and the Sea*, *Return to Castlevania*, and *The End is Near*. That track record of "live-service-style content drops inside a premium Metroidvania" is precisely why Konami partnered with them for Castlevania: Belmont's Curse Evil Empire. According to the [official Castlevania: Belmont's Curse Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4231820/Castlevania_Belmonts_Curse/), Evil Empire is listed as the developer, with Motion Twin credited as a creative consultant — a structure that mirrors the original Dead Cells arrangement before Evil Empire took the lead.

| Dead Cells Content Drop | Approximate Window | Evil Empire Role |
|---|---|---|
| Rise of the Giant | 2019 | Lead developer for free update |
| The Bad Seed | 2020 | Lead developer for paid DLC |
| Fatal Falls | 2021 | Lead developer for paid DLC |
| The Queen and the Sea | 2022 | Lead developer for paid DLC |
| Return to Castlevania | 2023 | Lead developer for crossover DLC |

That post-launch muscle is directly relevant to how Castlevania: Belmont's Curse is being scoped. Community chatter on the [Steam Community hub](https://steamcommunity.com/app/4231820/discussions/) already shows 246 active discussion threads covering boss rosters, custom mode achievement locking, regional pricing, and Switch 2 version requests — a sign that Evil Empire is comfortable managing a high-traffic, high-feedback launch community.

### Motion Twin's Advisory Role Explained

Motion Twin still owns the Dead Cells IP and is credited as a creative consultant on Castlevania: Belmont's Curse Evil Empire. In practice, that means Evil Empire has day-to-day control of the project, while Motion Twin signs off on the combat feel, run structure, and overall design language. The split is consistent with how the two studios worked on *Return to Castlevania*, the 2023 crossover that put Richter Belmont and Alucard into Dead Cells. That crossover is widely cited by community data as the proof-of-concept for this full collaboration, and the Belmont's Curse team has been open about carrying over lessons from it.

## Castlevania: Belmont's Curse 40th Anniversary Timing and Series Status

### Why the 40th Anniversary Matters for the 26th Entry

Castlevania: Belmont's Curse is positioned as the 26th mainline entry in a series that began in 1986, and its reveal is timed to the franchise's 40th anniversary celebrations. The 40th anniversary framing is more than a marketing banner — it gives Konami cover to take creative risks with a new protagonist, a new developer at the lead, and a brand-new engine architecture, because anniversary windows are the moments when legacy publishers historically relax their internal approval gates.

| Series Milestone | Year | Relevance to Belmont's Curse |
|---|---|---|
| Castlevania (NES) | 1986 | Series origin point |
| Castlevania III: Dracula's Curse | 1989 | Rose Belmont's father Trevor fights here |
| Castlevania: Curse of Darkness | 2005 | Continues the 1476–1499 timeline |
| Castlevania: Lords of Shadow | 2010 | 3D reboot, separate continuity |
| Dead Cells: Return to Castlevania | 2023 | Evil Empire's first Castlevania work |
| Castlevania: Belmont's Curse | 2026 | 26th mainline entry, 40th anniversary window |

The 40th anniversary hook also explains the dual-route, two-era structure seen in the official Steam description. Rose Belmont's 1499 adventure is set 23 years after Castlevania III: Dracula's Curse, which lets the writers pull on the Trevor/Sypha lineage while also nodding forward toward the 1476 timeline used in Curse of Darkness. That 23-year gap is a deliberate narrative bridge rather than an accident of scheduling, and it gives Evil Empire's writers room to thread in franchise deep cuts without breaking continuity.

### The 26th Mainline Entry Count Explained

The "26th entry" label is unusually precise for a Castlevania marketing push, and it matters because of how the franchise counts its own canon. Per the [Wikipedia entry on Castlevania: Belmont's Curse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castlevania:_Belmont%27s_Curse), Konami and Evil Empire are positioning the game as a direct continuation of the classic 2D timeline, not a spinoff or reboot. That distinction carries weight for long-time fans, because Lords of Shadow and Lords of Shadow 2 are typically excluded from the mainline count, and Netflix's *Castlevania* animated series has never been counted at all.

## How Castlevania: Belmont's Curse Gameplay Blends Dead Cells DNA with Classic Castlevania

### Combat, Whip, and the Vampire Killer Loop

Castlevania: Belmont's Curse Evil Empire leans hard into the Vampire Killer whip as both a weapon and a traversal tool, which is the clearest mechanical signal that this is a Metroidvania built on a Dead Cells-style skill curve rather than a classic linear Castlevania. The whip's reach and recovery frames are tuned for aggressive play, and the dual-route structure — medieval Paris plus the manifest Castle — gives Evil Empire room to alternate between tight urban combat and the wider, vertical exploration that the franchise is known for. The official Steam page highlights a "city + castle two-stage non-linear Metroidvania structure" with auto-map, save rooms, and fast-travel points all included from day one, which is a noticeable departure from older Castlevania titles that often gated basic quality-of-life behind progression.

### Build Craft, Progression, and the Dead Cells Carryover

The Dead Cells influence is most obvious in how Castlevania: Belmont's Curse Evil Empire handles its progression systems. The official Konami page describes a system where the Vampire Killer whip mutates alongside Rose's loadout, opening up build-craft branches that reward experimentation — much like the affix and synergy loops in *Return to Castlevania*. The pre-order Alucard costume is not just cosmetic: it ships with a distinct moveset modifier that changes how the whip behaves, giving early adopters a built-in second build to experiment with during the first run. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**Who is Evil Empire and why are they developing Castlevania: Belmont's Curse?**

Evil Empire is the French studio that has run Dead Cells live operations and shipped every major paid DLC since 2019, including *Return to Castlevania*. They are developing Castlevania: Belmont's Curse Evil Empire because Konami wanted a team fluent in fast, layered Metroidvania design, and Evil Empire proved that capability through the 2023 crossover. Motion Twin still owns Dead Cells and is credited as creative consultant on the new title.

**Is Castlevania: Belmont's Curse the 26th mainline Castlevania game?**

Yes. Konami and Evil Empire are positioning Castlevania: Belmont's Curse as the franchise's 26th mainline entry, set 23 years after Castlevania III: Dracula's Curse and continuing the classic 2D timeline. The 3D Lords of Shadow sub-series and the Netflix animated continuity are not counted in that number, which is why the official count lands on 26 rather than a higher total.

**What is the Castlevania: Belmont's Curse release date and launch time?**

The Castlevania: Belmont's Curse release date is October 15, 2026 across PC, PS5, Xbox Series X|S, and Switch. The Castlevania: Belmont's Curse launch time is expected to follow Konami's standard rolling-unlock pattern of 09:00 PT / 12:00 ET / 18:00 CEST, with the pre-order Alucard Style Costume Midnight Edition unlocking at the same moment.

**What does the Motion Twin and Evil Empire split mean for the game's design?**

Motion Twin retains creative oversight while Evil Empire runs day-to-day development, which is the same structure the two studios used on the 2023 *Return to Castlevania* crossover. The result is a Castlevania: Belmont's Curse Evil Empire build that feels mechanically snappy and run-tuned like Dead Cells, while still carrying the gothic tone, whip combat, and 2D exploration that define the mainline Castlevania series.

**Will there be a Switch 2 version or physical edition of Castlevania: Belmont's Curse?**

As of September 2026, only the standard Nintendo Switch version has been confirmed, and Steam Community threads are actively petitioning Evil Empire for a Switch 2 SKU. No boxed physical edition has been announced, but Konami has historically released collector's editions for major Castlevania anniversaries, so a 40th anniversary physical run is plausible but not confirmed.
