Castlevania: Belmont's Curse dropped a developer interview that finally pulls the curtain back on Evil Empire's Paris-set reimagining of the Castlevania legacy, and the timing matters because the demo window just opened to a divided fanbase. The conversation — featured during the recent **Xbox Games Showcase** and expanded across the studio's **dev diary** segments — confirms that this 2D action-exploration title from KONAMI, Evil Empire, and Motion Twin is the most ambitious collaboration the Dead Cells alumni have ever taken on. Below is a full breakdown of what the team said, what the **State of Play** trailer hinted at, and what players who tried the **demo** are actually reporting.

## What Evil Empire Revealed About the Belmont's Curse Vision

The **Castlevania: Belmont's Curse developer interview** opened with a clear thesis from the Evil Empire leadership: this is not a Dead Cells reskin with a Castlevania coat of paint. Producer Aymeric Music and Creative Director Sébastien Bénard stressed that the team wanted to honor the 2D Metroidvania DNA of Castlevania III: Dracula's Curse while layering the build-expression systems that Evil Empire refined across years of Dead Cells updates. The setting — **1499 Paris**, 23 years after Dracula's Curse — was chosen specifically because no prior Castlevania title had used the city as a fully explorable backdrop.

According to the team, **Castlevania: Belmont's Curse behind the scenes** decisions revolved around three pillars: a new protagonist in **Rose Belmont**, a non-linear city hub stitched together with catacombs and rooftops, and a weapon system that goes far beyond the Vampire Killer whip. The interview confirmed that the whip returns as Rose's signature armament, but it now shares weapon slots with spears, daggers, and holy relics — a direct response to long-running fan requests for build variety.

### Why Paris Over the Usual Castlevania Locales

The interview explained that the team deliberately avoided returning to **Wallachia** because the geography had been exhaustively charted across the series. **1499 Paris** offered a real-world city with verticality — bell towers, the Seine-side catacombs, and Notre-Dame's flying buttresses — that translated naturally into 2D platforming. This choice also lets Evil Empire weave historical fiction into the monster design, with demonic incursions layered onto real plague-era Parisian architecture.

| Setting Decision | Reason Given in Interview | Player Reaction |
|---|---|---|
| Paris over Wallachia | Fresh geography, vertical architecture | Strongly positive |
| 1499 (post-Dracula's Curse) | Bridges classic and modern timeline | Mostly positive |
| Rose Belmont as protagonist | New bloodline, not Trevor's descendant | Sharply divided |
| Non-linear city hub | Metroidvania tradition + modern design | Mostly positive |

## The Konami Collaboration and How It Shaped Development

A major slice of the **Castlevania: Belmont's Curse dev diary** coverage focused on the working relationship between Evil Empire, Motion Twin, and **KONAMI**. This is the first time KONAMI has entrusted an external Western studio with the core Castlevania brand in a generation, and the interview was unusually candid about the collaboration structure. Konami provided franchise bible access, character approvals, and lore review, while Evil Empire retained full creative control over combat, level design, and the roguelite-adjacent systems.

The interview confirmed that the **pre-order bonus** — the **Alucard Style Costume Midnight Edition** for Rose — was a Konami-driven inclusion meant to signal continuity with Castlevania's 1997 lineage. Evil Empire staff noted in the dev diary that getting Alucard's silhouette right on a Belmont-frame character required roughly four months of internal iteration.

### Creative Boundaries and Where Konami Drew Lines

According to community-reported summaries of the **State of Play** segment, KONAMI reserved veto power over three areas: the depiction of **Dracula**, the canonical fate of the Belmont bloodline, and any crossovers with non-Castlevania Konami IP. Evil Empire retained freedom on NPC design, side-quest framing, and the boss roster, which is why the team has been able to introduce new antagonists like **The Fallen** and historical figures such as **Joan of Arc** as boss-tier encounters. Players who cleared the demo specifically called out the **Joan of Arc boss** as a standout moment, and our breakdown of the encounter covers the dodge windows in detail over at the [how to beat Joan of Arc in Belmont's Curse guide](/en/bosses/how-to-beat-joan-of-arc-belmont-s-curse/).

| Collaboration Area | Konami Role | Evil Empire Role |
|---|---|---|
| Core lore / Dracula | Approval required | Full design |
| Combat system | Advisory | Full control |
| Boss roster | Lore review | Full design |
| Costumes / crossovers | Brand approval | Implementation |

## The State of Play and Xbox Games Showcase Reveals

The **Castlevania: Belmont's Curse State of Play** segment ran roughly seven minutes and focused on combat traversal rather than story cutscenes, a deliberate choice the team addressed in the interview. The **Xbox Games Showcase** version ran shorter — about three minutes — and centered on the Xbox Series X|S performance targets and the day-one inclusion in Xbox Game Pass. Both presentations confirmed that the title launches simultaneously on PC, PS5, Xbox Series X|S, and Switch 2.

The **Castlevania: Belmont's Curse Xbox Games Showcase** footage also confirmed a **60 FPS target on Series X** and **4K dynamic resolution**, while the Switch 2 build was shown running at a stable 30 FPS in handheld mode. Evil Empire's tech director noted that the Switch 2 version required a custom asset streaming pipeline because the cartridge-based install footprint is roughly 12 GB, with the rest pulled from a separate download.

### Demo Footage Breakdown and What Was Cut

Comparing the two presentations side by side reveals what the team prioritized. The **State of Play** build showed a longer Paris hub sequence with grappling-hook traversal, while the **Xbox Games Showcase** build cut that entirely in favor of a boss rush preview. According to the developer interview, the decision was made because Xbox's editorial team wanted to highlight encounter variety, while Sony's presentation slot favored environmental showcase.

## The Castlevania Belmont's Curse Demo: What's In It and How Players Reacted

The **Castlevania: Belmont's Curse demo** went live across PC, PS5, and Xbox Series X|S ahead of the full launch, and the reception has been deeply split in a way the developer interview actually anticipated. Evil Empire's community lead acknowledged in the dev diary that any Castlevania revival with a new protagonist — especially a non-Trevor Belmont — would draw polarized reactions, and the demo's forum threads confirm exactly that pattern.

According to community data aggregated from Steam discussions and Reddit, the demo's **first 30 minutes** cover Rose's arrival in burning Paris, a tutorial gauntlet against lesser demons, and a mid-difficulty boss fight against a corrupted cathedral knight. Players report that the **combat responsiveness** is the strongest element, with the parry window feeling tighter than Dead Cells in a way that rewards aggressive play.

### What the Demo Confirmed About the Weapon System

The demo's weapon drops confirmed what the developer interview teased: the whip is a baseline option, but **spear weapons** and **holy relics** meaningfully change the combat loop. Our dedicated [Castlevania Belmont's Curse spear weapons breakdown](/en/weapons/castlevania-belmont-s-curse-spear-weapons/) covers the specific reach-vs-damage tradeoffs, but the short version is that spears offer 30% more reach at the cost of a slower first-hit animation. Players who mained spears in the demo reported that they cleared the cathedral knight boss roughly 40 seconds faster than whip-only runs, based on community-uploaded clear times.

The demo also locked certain achievements behind the **custom mode** toggle, a feature Evil Empire addressed directly in the interview. The team explained that custom mode was originally designed as an accessibility layer for players who wanted to tweak enemy density and damage taken, but the achievement-locking mechanic was added late in development to preserve leaderboard integrity.

### The Divided Reaction Around Rose Belmont

The single most-discussed topic in **Castlevania: Belmont's Curse behind the scenes** community threads is **Rose Belmont** herself. Some players praised the **art direction** and the aggressive, shorter-frame character design, while others argued that the Belmont bloodline should have remained male-lineage canonical. Evil Empire addressed this in the interview by pointing out that **Rose's bloodline** is never specified as Trevor's direct descendant, leaving room for the broader Belmont clan to include female warriors.

## The Boss Roster and What the Interview Teased for the Full Game

The **Castlevania: Belmont's Curse developer interview** spent its final segment on the **boss roster**, which the team described as the single largest content investment in the project. According to Evil Empire, the game ships with **more than 20 boss encounters** spread across the main story, optional catacomb areas, and a **boss rush mode** unlocked after the first credits roll. The interview confirmed that the demo only shows a small fraction of this roster, and several of the more elaborate encounters are gated behind late-game progression.

The team also confirmed that **Dracula** appears in the game but stopped short of confirming whether he serves as the final boss. Community datamining of the demo files has surfaced a high-resolution Dracula silhouette, but Evil Empire's PR lead told the dev diary audience that players should treat leaks as speculative. Our analysis of [whether Dracula is the final boss in Belmont's Curse](/en/bosses/belmont-s-curse-is-dracula-the-final-boss/) pulls together the available evidence, but the definitive answer will only land with the full release.

### Optional Bosses and the Hidden Encounter Loop

Beyond the main story bosses, the interview revealed that **optional bosses** are tied to specific weapon upgrade paths and hidden NPCs scattered across Paris. **Medusa**, **Carmilla**, **Isaac**, and **The Fallen** were all name-checked as optional encounters with bespoke arenas. Each one drops a unique weapon modifier that feeds into the build-expression systems Evil Empire is known for. The [Castlevania Belmont's Curse optional bosses catalog](/en/bosses/castlevania-belmont-s-curse-optional-bosses/) tracks every confirmed encounter and the prerequisites for unlocking them.

Evil Empire noted that several NPCs in the demo are actually **allies** rather than enemies, a design choice the team made to avoid the common roguelite problem of disposable side characters.

## What to Watch Before the Full Launch

The **Castlevania: Belmont's Curse developer interview** ended on a forward-looking note, with Evil Empire confirming that the team is already planning a **post-launch roadmap** that includes additional bosses, a Nightmare difficulty tier, and at least one free story chapter set in a different European city. The team also addressed **regional pricing feedback** that surfaced in the Steam community, stating that Evil Empire is in active talks with KONAMI to adjust launch pricing in markets where the standard Castlevania premium has historically been a barrier.

Players waiting on a **Switch 2 physical edition** will need to wait a bit longer — the interview confirmed that the Switch 2 build launches digitally first, with the cartridge version following roughly six weeks later due to manufacturing lead times. Evil Empire also confirmed that **cross-save between PC and consoles** is supported through KONAMI ID, but cross-progression between Switch 2 and other platforms is gated behind a separate opt-in.

The combination of the demo reception, the State of Play coverage, and the developer interview paints a picture of a Castlevania revival that is taking real creative risks while honoring the franchise's 2D Metroidvania roots. Whether Rose Belmont wins over the skeptics will likely come down to how the full game's story lands, but the combat and weapon systems are already proving strong enough to carry the title through launch regardless.

Console demos typically close one to two weeks before launch, so players should grab it soon if they want to sample the weapon variety before the full release.

**How long is the Castlevania: Belmont's Curse demo?**

The demo covers approximately the first 30 minutes of the game, including the opening Paris sequence, a tutorial gauntlet, and one mid-difficulty boss fight. Evil Empire designed it to showcase the combat responsiveness and the spear-versus-whip weapon tradeoffs rather than the full city hub exploration.

**Will the full game have more bosses than the demo shows?**

Yes. The developer interview confirmed that the full Castlevania: Belmont's Curse ships with more than 20 boss encounters across main story, optional, and boss rush categories, while the demo only exposes a small fraction. Several late-game bosses are gated behind progression and will not appear in any pre-release build.

**Is Rose Belmont related to Trevor Belmont from Castlevania III?**

The developer interview deliberately left Rose's exact bloodline unspecified, confirming only that she carries the Belmont name and the whip-bearing heritage. Evil Empire stated in the dev diary that the team wanted flexibility to explore the broader Belmont clan rather than locking Rose into a strict Trevor-descendant role, which is why some fans read the relationship as ambiguous on purpose.
